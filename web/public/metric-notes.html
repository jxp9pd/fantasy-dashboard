Fantasy football metric notes

Short reading notes for the half-PPR WR and RB season dashboards. Route-based metrics are unavailable from the free data sources used in this version.

Shared scoring measures

  • Points/game: Who has produced when playing? Touchdowns can move this sharply, so inspect the workload underneath.
  • Expected points/game (xFP): How valuable were the opportunities received? This describes past workload, not next-week projected scoring. A gap versus actual points can reflect conversion, skill, offensive context, or noise; it is not an automatic buy/sell signal.

How xFP/G is calculated

xFP/G means expected fantasy points per game. It estimates the fantasy value of the opportunities a player actually received in completed games. A target that falls incomplete still has opportunity value; a carry near the goal line generally has more touchdown potential than a carry at midfield. The dashboard uses the published ffverse/ffopportunity model outputs.

1. The provider values individual plays

ffopportunity describes its model as XGBoost models trained on nflverse play-by-play from 2006–2020. Separate fitted models estimate pass completion probability, yards after catch, passing/receiving touchdown probability, interception probability, rushing yards, and rushing touchdown probability. First-down models also exist, but first downs earn no points under this dashboard’s scoring rules.

The upstream preprocessing supplies play context such as field position, down and yards to go, air yards, pass direction, run direction/gap, player position, shotgun/no-huddle, score and time remaining, weather, and betting-derived game context. Each fitted model has its own predictor blueprint; this is a learned model, so there is no single fixed “points per target” or “points per carry” multiplier.

For an ordinary receiving target, the half-PPR scoring relationship is: 

Expected receiving yards = P(catch) × (air yards + expected yards after catch)
Target xFP = 0.5 × P(catch)
           + 0.1 × expected receiving yards
           + 6 × P(receiving touchdown)

The touchdown probability is already a probability for that target; it is not multiplied by catch probability a second time. The provider predicts completion and yards after catch first, uses those outputs when estimating touchdowns, and gives special treatment to passes whose air yards equal the distance to the goal line.

For an ordinary rushing attempt:

Carry xFP = 0.1 × expected rushing yards
          + 6 × P(rushing touchdown)

The provider also scores two-point opportunities using conversion probabilities, and includes a player’s passing contributions where present: 0.04 per expected passing yard, 4 per expected passing touchdown, −2 per expected interception, and 2 per expected two-point conversion. Its special-play handling covers conversions, kneels, aborted runs, and spikes. These formulas explain ordinary targets and carries; the imported provider total remains the authoritative input for those special cases.

2. We convert the published weekly totals to half-PPR

The build reads ep_weekly_2026.parquet from the provider’s latest-data release. That file already sums each player’s receiving, rushing, and passing expected points for each game under full-PPR scoring. 

Weekly half-PPR xFP
  = total_fantasy_points_exp − 0.5 × receptions_exp

The subtraction uses expected receptions. Actual catches are used only in the separate actual-points conversion: total_fantasy_points − 0.5 × receptions. No return touchdowns or first-down bonuses are added.

3. We divide by the player’s games played

xFP/G = sum of weekly half-PPR xFP in the selected period
        ÷ games played in that period

Season includes all eligible appearances through the common completed-week data cutoff. Last 4 games includes that player’s four most recent appearances, even if a bye or absence makes them span more than four calendar weeks. A game counts when the player was active on the weekly roster or recorded any snaps and the team’s game was completed. Zero-target, zero-carry, and special-teams-only appearances count in the denominator; byes and inactive weeks do not.

If a player has a counted appearance but no individual ffopportunity row in an otherwise covered game, this dashboard assigns zero actual points and zero xFP for that appearance. That rule assumes no recorded modeled opportunity; an upstream missing-player-row problem would also appear as zero. Whole games missing from a required source are excluded by the shared data cutoff.

A worked example from the current snapshot

In the snapshot built October 4, 2026 at 9:05 PM Pacific, Jahmyr Gibbs has three included games:

(30.95 + 19.07 + 27.545) ÷ 3 = 25.855 xFP/G, displayed as 25.9. This is a dated calculation example; later refreshes can change the live season value.

How to interpret the number

xFP describes the value of observed opportunities using historical outcomes. It does not predict next week’s workload, and the actual-minus-expected gap can reflect execution, skill, context, and model limitations. A player with more valuable targets or goal-line carries can have high xFP despite low actual scoring.

Two implementation details matter: the provider’s expected-point formulas contain no expected-fumble deduction, while its actual rushing/receiving points include lost-fumble penalties. Also, the provider rounds weekly component sums to two decimals before publishing. The dashboard keeps the precision available in those fields through conversion, aggregation, and sorting, and rounds to one decimal only for display.

Method reviewed October 5, 2026 against the provider’s published v1.0.0 data version and upstream commit 74dcb35. The app consumes the provider’s latest-data feed and records its source timestamp; it does not train these models locally.

Wide receivers

  • Targets/game: How much receiving volume is available before catches happen?
  • Target share: How central is the player to the passing offense? Pair it with targets/game because teams throw at different rates.
  • Route participation: Is the player consistently getting chances to earn targets? A growing route role can precede stronger production.
  • Yards/route: Does the player turn routes into yardage? Read alongside route count; a few long catches can distort a small sample.
  • Targets/route: Does the player earn targets when running routes? Helps distinguish limited playing time from limited target earning.
  • End-zone targets/game: Is the player getting direct touchdown opportunities? These are scarce, so avoid overreacting to one week.
  • First downs/route: Does the player repeatedly generate useful receiving production? Complements yards/route, but the two overlap heavily.

Reading pattern: Rising route participation and target share suggest an expanding role. Rising points with flat opportunity invite a closer look at touchdowns and big plays.

Running backs

  • Carries/game: How much rushing volume does the player receive? Game script can change this without changing backfield status.
  • Targets/game: How involved is the player as a receiver? Receiving work adds half-PPR value and can support production when the team trails.
  • Share of RB carries: How much of the backfield workload does the player control? Pair the percentage with absolute carries.
  • Carries inside the five/game: Is the player receiving valuable rushing touchdown opportunities?
  • Route participation: Is the player part of the passing offense? Pass-blocking snaps do not provide the same target opportunity.
  • Share of team inside-five carries: Who controls goal-line rushing work, including competition from the quarterback? Inspect the count as well as the percentage.
  • Yards after contact/carry: Supporting evidence of rushing performance. Blocking, opponents, and charting affect it; keep workload primary.

Reading pattern: Growing carry share plus receiving involvement suggests a broader role. Goal-line work adds scoring potential. High scoring from a narrow, touchdown-heavy role can be more volatile.

Across both positions

Compare season results with the last four appearances and inspect the weekly timeline. Check sample size, injuries, teammate availability, quarterback changes, and charting coverage before interpreting a trend. Recent usage is evidence of a role change, not a guarantee it will persist.
